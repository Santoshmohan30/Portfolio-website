I was trained as an engineer and came to neuroscience through its data. I now work across the whole research chain: stereotaxic surgery, perfusion and histology, behavioral experiments, calcium imaging and fiber photometry, and the AI/ML models that turn recordings into understanding. I want to learn how early-life experience reshapes the circuits that teach the brain to expect reward, and to build the tools that make those changes measurable.

Research Interests

  • Early-life stress and reward learning: how adversity early in life changes the way animals learn to expect reward.
  • Dopamine, expectation and motivation: neural signals of expectation and outcome, interpreted through learning models such as Rescorla–Wagner.
  • Quantitative behavior: 3D tracking of the face and body as a richer readout of behavioral state than licks or lever presses.
  • Computational models of behavior: reinforcement learning, inverse reinforcement learning and multi-agent models of interacting animals.
  • Systems neuroscience methods: miniature two-photon calcium imaging, single- and multi-region fiber photometry, circuit manipulation.

FaceRecon: 3D Reconstruction of Mouse Facial Behavior

NYSPI · 2026 – Present

  • Reproduced two published facial-behavior pipelines (keypoint tracking and HOG-based facial-expression analysis) to benchmark existing approaches.
  • Synchronized multi-camera rig; the face is segmented with the Segment Anything Model, hundreds of skin points are tracked and triangulated into a moving 3D mesh.

Computational Modeling of Multi-Agent Animal Behavior

Roy Lab · 2025 – 2026

  • Transformer-based conditional variational autoencoder forecasting the movement of interacting mice from pose tracks (SLEAP, 1M+ frames).
  • Behavior cloning with maximum-entropy inverse reinforcement learning; 3.5× better than a constant-velocity baseline.
  • Key lesson: reproducing movement is not explaining it, since the model ignored the partner's state, which points toward multi-agent RL.
